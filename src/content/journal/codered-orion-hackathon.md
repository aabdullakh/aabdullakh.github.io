I went to CodeRED Orion, the hackathon hosted by UH, expecting to spend the weekend building. My team, Vector, built Flood Father, a voice assistant for Houston that answers flood questions out loud using live USGS stream gauges and National Weather Service alerts. We didn't win. It was still one of the best things I've done this year.

## Pitching beat building

I like building and shipping, but the thing I took away most was how good pitching is. Standing in front of people and explaining what you made, and why you care about it, is a skill I didn't know I wanted until I tried it.

Explaining the project out loud to people seeing it for the first time forced me to answer questions I hadn't asked myself. Why voice instead of another dashboard? Why not just use Gemini? What happens when the data is missing? How did you use Vultr?

My answer to the Gemini question was simple. When a flood hits, the internet may be down, and a Spanish-speaking grandmother may never have used an AI chatbot. A phone call to one number is the safest thing that works. A general chatbot also can't read a live stream gauge or tell you how old the reading is. Ours does.

## The idea matters

I saw a lot of projects. There were 97 teams, and the ones that stuck with me did one of two things: they were genuinely cool, or they helped real people. The best ones did both. If you're going to spend a weekend on something, pick an idea worth talking about.

## The sponsor conversation

Sponsors were there too, and one of them was Reynolds and Reynolds, a company I had never heard of before. We pitched our project to one of their representatives. The following week I applied for their Software Engineering Internship, and my resume made it through to the next round. It was the first time that has ever happened to me. I'm now moving on to interviews.

I'm not saying a hackathon guarantees anything. But a short conversation about something real we built gave me a way in that a cold application doesn't.

## What I actually like

What excites me is building something people can use. I think of it like a museum. I enjoy walking through and seeing different art and visuals, and I get bored when there's only text on a wall. A website or app you can open and try is the same thing: you don't have to take my word for it, you can use it.

That's why I like ideas that connect to the real world. Flood Father is live if you want to try it: [thefloodfather.surf](https://thefloodfather.surf). The write-up is on [DevPost](https://devpost.com/software/the-flood-father), and the technical breakdown is on my [projects page](/projects/).

## What's next

We didn't win, but I came away with feedback on how to build better projects and what it takes to win next time.
